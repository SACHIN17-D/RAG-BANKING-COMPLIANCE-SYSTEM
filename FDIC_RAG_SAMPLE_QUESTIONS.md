> 
> **User Query**: *"I am applying for a commercial loan with $450k revenue. Why does the bank require 3 years of tax returns and cash flow statements to determine my eligibility?"*

- **Grounded Answer**: 
  - According to FDIC guidelines (Page 3, Page 15), commercial loan underwriting policies strictly mandate obtaining comprehensive credit information—including operating statements, personal financial statements, and verified cash-flow data—before evaluating creditworthiness.
  - For term loans and commercial credit, underwriters must analyze historical cash flows to verify that the borrower has sufficient long-term debt-service capacity.

### Question 3: Accounts Receivable Line of Credit & Overdue Invoices
> **Applicant Scenario**: *"My manufacturing business has $300,000 in outstanding customer invoices, but $80,000 of those invoices are 90+ days overdue. When I applied for an accounts receivable line of credit, the bank excluded the $80,000 from my borrowing base. Why?"*
> 
> **User Query**: *"I have $300k in receivables but $80k is 90 days overdue. Why did the bank exclude the overdue invoices from my loan borrowing base?"*

- **FDIC Reference**: Page 13
- **Grounded Answer**: 
  - Under FDIC Accounts Receivable Financing rules (Page 13), banks can only extend credit against "acceptable receivables."
  - Receivable eligibility is strictly determined by age and turnover. Invoices past 90 days are systematically excluded from the borrowing base to protect against collateral dilution and uncollectible credit losses.

## 4. Governance, Loan Approvals & Working Capital

> 
> **User Query**: *"My loan officer promised my business loan would be approved, but the credit committee rejected it. Who has final loan approval authority under FDIC rules?"*

- **FDIC Reference**: Page 5 & Page 6
- **Grounded Answer**: 
  - Yes. FDIC guidelines (Page 5, Page 6) require banks to maintain an independent credit review system separate from loan officers to avoid conflicts of interest.
  - Final approval authority rests with independent credit committees or senior risk officers, ensuring all significant loans are evaluated objectively regardless of loan officer promises.

### Question 13: Past-Due Loan Payments & Non-Accrual Status
> **Applicant Scenario**: *"I missed two monthly payments on my commercial loan and am 75 days past due. My loan officer warned that if I reach 90 days past due, the loan will be placed on 'non-accrual status.' What does non-accrual status mean under FDIC rules?"*
> 
> **User Query**: *"I am 75 days past due on my loan. What FDIC rules govern placing a loan on non-accrual status at 90 days past due?"*

- **FDIC Reference**: Page 36
- **Grounded Answer**: 
  - Under FDIC rules (Page 36), loans that are 90 days or more past due must be placed on non-accrual status unless they are well-secured and in the process of collection.
  - Non-accrual status indicates heightened credit risk, requiring the bank to stop recognizing unpaid interest as income and re-evaluate loss allowances.
